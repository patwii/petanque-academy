## Executive Summary

Self-awareness is the meta-skill that accelerates all other development. Players with accurate self-perception improve faster because they train what actually needs work. The challenge: those with the weakest self-awareness often believe they have excellent self-knowledge. This module addresses the "self-awareness catch-22" with practical tools for objective self-assessment.

| **Plateaued players** | Identify blind spots preventing progress |

### Learning Objectives

After completing this module, players will be able to:
1. Explain why self-perception is often inaccurate (and why this matters)
2. Use the Johari Window framework to identify blind spots
3. Request and receive feedback effectively
4. Use video analysis for objective self-assessment
5. Create a personal development plan based on accurate self-knowledge

## What Is Self-Awareness (In Sports)?

### Definition for Athletes
- Accurate perception of your own abilities
- Understanding how others experience you
- Recognizing patterns in your behavior
- Knowing your emotional triggers

### Self-Awareness vs Self-Criticism
⚠️ CRITICAL DISTINCTION
- Self-awareness: "I notice I tense up in pressure situations"
- Self-criticism: "I always choke under pressure, I'm weak"
- One leads to improvement, the other to spiral

## The Johari Window for Athletes

### Mermaid Diagram: Adapted Johari Window

[2x2 grid showing:
  - Open (Known to self, Known to others): Your recognized strengths/weaknesses
  - Blind Spot (Unknown to self, Known to others): What teammates see that you don't
  - Hidden (Known to self, Unknown to others): Private struggles
  - Unknown (Unknown to both): Untapped potential]

### Quadrant Deep-Dive

#### 1. Open Area (Arena)
- Your acknowledged skills
- Known weaknesses you're working on
- GOAL: Expand this quadrant

#### 2. Blind Spot (The Critical One)
- Technical habits you don't notice
- Body language you're unaware of
- Emotional patterns others see
- This is where feedback is gold

#### 3. Hidden Area (Facade)
- Private doubts and fears
- Struggles you hide from teammates
- When to share vs when to protect

#### 4. Unknown (Potential)
- Capabilities you haven't discovered
- How coaches help uncover this

## Why Self-Perception Fails

### The Dunning-Kruger Effect in Sports
- Low skill + low awareness = dangerous overconfidence
- The "unconscious incompetence" trap
- Why beginners often think they're better than they are

### The Expert's Blind Spot
- High skill can also mean blind spots
- "It's always worked" prevents evolution
- The curse of success

### Emotional Interference
- We see what we want to see
- Memory distortion after matches
- The highlight reel effect

## The Self-Awareness Paradox

### The Catch-22
- Those who need more self-awareness typically think they have enough
- Those with high self-awareness constantly question themselves
- How do you know which you are?

### The Solution: External Mirrors
- You need objective feedback sources
- Video doesn't lie
- Statistics don't flatter
- Trusted truth-tellers

## Benefits of Accurate Self-Knowledge

### Faster Improvement
- Train what actually needs work
- Stop wasting time on imaginary problems
- Prioritize correctly

### Better Team Dynamics
- Understand your impact on others
- Accept feedback without defensiveness
- Build trust through openness

### Smarter Competition Strategy
- Know your real strengths (play to them)
- Know your real weaknesses (protect them)
- Make realistic tactical decisions

# Getting and Using Objective Feedback

## Why Your Own Perception Isn't Enough
- The mirror is always distorted
- Memory reconstructs, it doesn't record
- Emotions color everything

## Sources of Objective Feedback

### 1. Teammates and Training Partners
**Advantages:**
- See you regularly
- Understand the game
- Know your patterns

**Challenges:**
- May not want to hurt feelings
- Their own biases
- May lack vocabulary

### 2. Coaches and Mentors
**Advantages:**
- Trained to observe
- Compare you to many players
- Can be direct

**Challenges:**
- May not see everyday patterns
- Their style preference
- Limited time

### 3. Video Analysis
**Advantages:**
- 100% objective
- Can review repeatedly
- Reveals unconscious habits

**Challenges:**
- Can be overwhelming
- Need to know what to look for
- Technical setup required

[Link to video analysis page]

### 4. Statistics and Tracking
**Advantages:**
- Numbers don't lie
- Track trends over time
- Compare to benchmarks

**Challenges:**
- What to measure?
- Context matters
- Can miss qualitative factors

## How to Ask for Feedback

### The Wrong Way
❌ "How did I play?"
❌ "Was I good?"
❌ "What do you think?"

### The Right Way
✅ "What's one thing I could improve in my pointing?"
✅ "Did you notice any pattern in my misses today?"
✅ "How do I come across to opponents when we're losing?"
✅ "What would you work on if you were me?"

### Specific Questions by Area

#### Technical Feedback
- "What do you notice about my release?"
- "Is my routine consistent?"
- "Where does my body language change?"

#### Mental Game Feedback
- "How do I seem under pressure?"
- "Do I look confident?"
- "What happens to my energy after a miss?"

#### Team Dynamics Feedback
- "Am I supportive enough?"
- "Do I communicate clearly?"
- "What's it like to play with me?"

## How to Receive Feedback

### The Defensive Trap
Natural reactions to critical feedback:
- Explain/justify
- Deflect to others
- Minimize ("it was just one time")
- Counter-attack ("well, you also...")

### The Productive Response
1. **Listen fully** (don't interrupt)
2. **Thank them** (this was hard for them too)
3. **Clarify** ("Can you give me an example?")
4. **Reflect** (don't respond immediately)
5. **Act** (or consciously decide not to)

### Managing Your Emotions
- It's normal to feel defensive
- Take a breath before responding
- Separate the message from the messenger
- Sleep on it before deciding

## Creating a Feedback Culture

### In Your Team
- Normalize giving and receiving feedback
- Make it regular, not special occasions
- Celebrate improvement, not just results
- Lead by asking for feedback first

### The Feedback Agreement
Template for training partners:
"I want to improve. I give you permission to tell me:
- When I'm making the same mistake repeatedly
- When my body language is negative
- When I'm affecting the team negatively
I promise to receive it without defensiveness."

## Practical Tools

### Monthly Feedback Request Template
Send to 2-3 trusted people:

"I'm working on my self-awareness as a player. Could you answer these 3 questions honestly?

1. What's my biggest strength that I should use more?
2. What's one thing holding me back that I might not see?
3. If you were my coach, what would you have me work on?

Thank you - your honesty helps me improve."

### Post-Match Feedback Protocol
After important matches, ask one specific question:
- "What did you notice about my game today?"
- Compare their answer to your self-assessment
- Note the gaps

| Date | Source | Feedback Received | My Reaction | Action Taken |
|------|--------|------------------|-------------|--------------|

# Video Analysis for Self-Discovery

## The Unbiased Mirror
- Opening: "The camera doesn't lie, flatter, or criticize"
- Why video reveals what you can't feel
- The gap between self-perception and reality
- Most players are shocked by their first video review

## What Video Reveals

### Technical Habits You Don't Notice
- Release point variations
- Balance shifts
- Grip pressure changes (visible in hand)
- Inconsistent follow-through

### Body Language Under Pressure
- Shoulder position changes
- Facial expressions
- Pace changes (rushed vs deliberate)
- Posture shifts (confident vs defeated)

### Pre-Shot Routine Consistency
- Timing variations you don't feel
- Steps you skip under pressure
- Rituals that disappear when nervous

### Team Communication Patterns
- Who initiates communication
- Body orientation during discussions
- Energy exchange between teammates
- Non-verbal reactions to teammates' shots

## What to Record

### Match Recording
**Advantages:**
- Real pressure situations
- See actual competition behavior
- True patterns emerge

**What to capture:**
- Full throws, not just results
- Between-throw behavior
- Team interactions
- Pressure moments especially

### Training Recording
**Advantages:**
- Controlled environment
- Can repeat and compare
- Lower stakes for review

**What to capture:**
- Technique work sessions
- Pressure drill performance
- Routine practice

### Simple Setup

#### Equipment Needed
- Smartphone (sufficient quality)
- Tripod or stable mount
- Wide enough angle to capture full motion
- Good audio (captures self-talk, team talk)

#### Camera Positions
1. **Behind and side** - Best for technique
2. **Front** - Body language, facial expressions
3. **Wide team shot** - Communication patterns

## How to Review Video

### The First Watch: General Impression
- Watch without analysis first
- Notice your emotional reaction
- What stands out immediately?
- Don't pause or rewind

### The Second Watch: Technical Focus
- Pause at key moments
- Frame-by-frame for release point
- Compare successful vs unsuccessful throws
- Look for patterns, not individual errors

### The Third Watch: Mental/Behavioral Focus
- Body language analysis
- Routine consistency check
- Reaction to outcomes
- Team interaction patterns

### What NOT to Do
- ❌ Watch immediately after match (emotions too raw)
- ❌ Focus only on mistakes
- ❌ Watch without specific questions
- ❌ Compare yourself to elite players unfairly

## Comparing Self-Perception to Video Reality

### The Exercise
1. After a match, write down how you think you performed (specific observations)
2. Record: "I think my body language was [confident/neutral/negative]"
3. "I think my routine was [consistent/mostly consistent/inconsistent]"
4. Then watch video
5. Compare your predictions to reality
6. Note the gaps

### Common Discoveries
- "I thought I was confident, but I looked nervous"
- "I didn't realize I rush my routine when losing"
- "I had no idea my shoulders rise before difficult shots"
- "I look more negative than I feel"

### Using the Gaps
- Gaps reveal blind spots
- Focus future training on actual issues
- Adjust self-perception calibration

## Advanced: Periodic Video Reviews

### Monthly Video Audit
- Record one match per month
- Same analysis protocol
- Track changes over time
- Build video library of progress

### Pre-Competition Review
- Watch your best performances
- Reinforce positive patterns
- Not for criticism, for confidence

### Season Review
- Compare early vs late season
- Identify improvements
- Set next season goals based on evidence

## Sharing Video with Others

### With Coaches
- Ask specific questions before sharing
- "What do you notice about my [X]?"
- Be open to insights

### With Teammates
- Great for team communication analysis
- "How can we communicate better?"
- Build trust through shared vulnerability

### Privacy Considerations
- Get consent for match recordings
- Club policies may apply
- Opponent visibility in video

### Body Language Decoder
**Confident patterns:**
- Level shoulders
- Steady pace
- Eye contact with target
- Smooth, deliberate movements

**Nervous patterns:**
- Raised shoulders
- Rushed movements
- Avoiding eye contact
- Fidgeting, adjusting

**Defeated patterns:**
- Dropped posture
