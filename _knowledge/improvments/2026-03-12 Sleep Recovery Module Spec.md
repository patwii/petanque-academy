## Executive Summary

Sleep is the most underrated performance factor in precision sports. Unlike endurance athletes who can "push through" fatigue, pétanque players require steady hands, sharp decision-making, and emotional regulation—all of which degrade rapidly with sleep deficit. This module will establish comprehensive sleep education as a core competitive advantage.

# Sleep Hygiene for Athletes

## The 10 Sleep Hygiene Fundamentals

### 1. Consistent Schedule
- Same wake time every day (±30 min)
- Even on weekends
- The anchor that sets your rhythm

### 2. Morning Light Exposure
- Bright light within 30 min of waking
- Natural light > artificial
- Sets your circadian clock

### 3. Caffeine Timing
- No caffeine after 2pm (or 8-10 hours before bed)
- Half-life is 5-6 hours
- Hidden sources: chocolate, pre-workout

### 4. Alcohol Awareness
- Alcohol disrupts sleep architecture
- Fragments REM sleep
- Worse recovery even if you "pass out"

### 5. Temperature Control
- Cool room: 18-20°C (65-68°F)
- Body temperature needs to drop
- Hot bath 90 min before bed can help

### 6. Darkness Matters
- Complete darkness for sleep
- Blue light disruption in evening
- Use dim, warm lights after sunset

### 7. Exercise Timing
- Regular exercise improves sleep
- But not within 3-4 hours of bedtime
- Morning/afternoon training ideal

### 8. Food Timing
- No large meals within 3 hours of bed
- Light protein snack OK if needed
- Avoid sugar in evening

### 9. Wind-Down Routine
- 30-60 min buffer before bed
- Same activities each night
- Signal to brain: sleep is coming

### 10. Bedroom = Sleep Only
- Remove work, TV, screens
- Brain association matters
- The "cave" environment

## Mermaid Diagram: Sleep Environment Optimization

[Environment diagram showing:
- Temperature: 18-20°C
- Light: Complete darkness
- Sound: Quiet or consistent white noise
- Devices: Out of room or face-down]

## Evening Routine Template

### 2 Hours Before Bed
- Finish eating
- Dim household lights
- Finish any work tasks

### 1 Hour Before Bed
- Stop screens (or use blue light filters)
- Light stretching or reading
- Prepare for tomorrow

### 30 Minutes Before Bed
- Personal hygiene routine
- Bedroom only
- Relaxation practice (PMR, breathing)

### In Bed
- No phones
- If awake > 20 min, get up briefly
- Trust the process

## Screen Time: A Practical Approach

### The Problem
- Blue light suppresses melatonin
- Content stimulates the mind
- "Just one more" scroll

### Realistic Solutions
- Blue light glasses after sunset
- Night mode on all devices
- Hard cutoff 30-60 min before bed
- Keep phone outside bedroom

## Sleep Tracking: Helpful or Harmful?

### Potential Benefits
- Awareness of patterns
- Correlation with performance
- Motivation for improvement

### Potential Harms
- "Orthosomnia" - anxiety about sleep
- Inaccurate data creates stress
- Checking app disrupts sleep

### Recommendation
- Track for 2-3 weeks to find patterns
- Then trust your routines
- Don't obsess over nightly data

## The 30-Day Sleep Challenge

### Week 1: Foundation
- Set consistent wake time
- Morning light exposure
- Track current baseline

### Week 2: Environment
- Optimize bedroom temperature
- Improve darkness
- Remove screens

### Week 3: Routine
- Establish wind-down routine
- Caffeine audit and cutoff
- Evening meal timing

### Week 4: Refinement
- Fine-tune based on data
- Address remaining issues
- Build long-term habits

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### "I can't fall asleep"
- Establish consistent wake time first
- Add wind-down routine
- Check caffeine timing
- Consider relaxation techniques

### "I wake up at 3am"
- Often stress/anxiety related
- Check alcohol consumption
- Try journaling before bed
- Don't look at clock

### "I don't feel rested"
- Track actual sleep vs time in bed
- Check for sleep apnea if snoring
- Improve sleep environment
- Consider sleep study

### "I'm too busy to sleep"
- Reframe: Sleep is performance enhancement
- Calculate true cost of sleep deprivation
- Start with just 20 min more

## Resources & Tools

### Recommended Apps
- Sleep tracking: Sleep Cycle, Oura
- Meditation: Headspace, Calm
- White noise: White Noise, Rain Rain

### Further Reading
- Walker, M. (2017). *Why We Sleep*
- Littlehales, N. (2016). *Sleep: The Myth of 8 Hours*
